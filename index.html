AI ACCESS OBSERVATORY

Africa’s ability to participate in the AI economy — measured, not reported.

Africa is entering a period of rapid technological transformation, driven by artificial intelligence, automation and digital infrastructure. Most of what’s written about it is fragmented, produced for a global audience, and difficult to verify from an African vantage point.

This record exists to close that gap. It examines Africa’s ability to participate in the AI economy across three dimensions — Jobs, Access and Infrastructure — and keeps the evidence as data: sourced, dated and status-tagged, row by row.

Each row is a single verifiable claim — a price, a megawatt, a signal — carrying its source, its evidence status, and the date it last moved.

Built for policymakers, researchers, journalists and development institutions who need a number they can verify, not a headline they have to trust.

What we don’t know

Stated as counts, generated from the registers. This is the part of the record that most sites leave out.

  • 16 of 22 infrastructure rows carry no published megawatt figure.
  • 2 of 13 priced models have no score on the restated capability index, so they are not plotted: DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731, Muse Spark 1.1.
  • 26 of 130 affordability rows have no minimum-wage figure, because Ethiopia has no private-sector minimum wage and Rwanda sets sectoral rates only.
  • No African provider or locally hosted inference is priced.
  • No jobs rows are structurally extracted, so there are no country totals, no headcount and no wage data.
  • 11 register rows have no archive snapshot of their source; 8 point at a bare domain or a session container and are quarantined.

Known gaps

Where one measure stops, the other does not

The full registers cover 33 countries for the employment-weighted index and 36 for task share. Within the original ten-country comparison sample, the employment-weighted index covers 6: Côte d'Ivoire, Egypt, Ethiopia, Ghana, Kenya, Rwanda. Morocco, Nigeria, Senegal, South Africa are not in its baseline sample, which excludes 26 countries for employment-data quality. The task-share measure covers all ten. Where a country is missing from a measure, that measure shows nothing for it; this record does not transplant the other measure's value into the gap.

The feed is still not a register of jobs

Exposure is now measured on two constructions; employment is not. There are still no rows carrying country, employer, headcount and wage from reported events, so the Jobs pillar cannot yet say how many jobs were created or lost, or what they paid.

What neither exposure figure can tell you

Exposure is not displacement. A job can be highly exposed and grow, or barely exposed and vanish. These layers say which labour markets sit closest to the technology, not what has happened to them.

Exposure across the continent

Two published measures, shown separately and never on one axis, because they do not measure the same thing. The task-share source has values for 36 of Africa's 54 countries; the employment-weighted source covers 33. Missing country values remain “No information”.

The second measure, on a different construction

This one weights by employment structure instead of holding the task universe fixed, so it answers a different question and covers fewer countries.

What these measures are, and what they are not

Neither is a share of jobs, and they are not interchangeable

The task-share measure asks what fraction of work tasks could plausibly be automated once each task is assessed under that country's conditions. The index asks how exposed the average job is, once you weight each occupation by how many people actually do it in that country. A country can rank differently on the two: South Africa leads the task-share ranking among African countries at 51.0%, while the employment-weighted index puts Mauritius first at 0.289. Both orderings are correct answers to different questions, which is why they are drawn on separate axes.

The index answers a different question from the share on the homepage

The homepage carries the IMF's finding that about four-fifths of jobs in sub-Saharan Africa have limited exposure to AI, which implies roughly one job in five sits in an exposed occupation. That is a share of jobs. Neither measure here is. Three quantities, three sources, never mixed.

Both are modelled estimates

Nobody observes a country's exposure directly. Both are computed by their authors from published inputs, which is why they carry a status of their own: modelled, meaning computed from published inputs, neither observed nor a projection. Their evidence is research papers, which is also its own category in this record.
